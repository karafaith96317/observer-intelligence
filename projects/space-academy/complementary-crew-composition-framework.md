# Complementary Crew Composition, Faculty & Human Performance Framework

Status: supporting research architecture for SA-CONCEPT-002. This document defines testable program-design hypotheses; it is not an established selection doctrine, demographic ranking system, or claim of participation by any named agency.

## 1. Core proposition

The Academy should evaluate both individual capability and deliberately composed team performance.

Primary question:

> Which combinations of demonstrated capabilities, cognitive approaches, operational experience, communication styles, physical abilities, cultural/language competencies, and domain expertise produce the most resilient collective performance when teams encounter unfamiliar, stressful, degraded, or resource-constrained problems?

The optimization target is not a crew of uniformly high scorers. It is a crew system with capability coverage, redundancy, complementarity, communication compatibility, adaptive leadership, and failure tolerance.

## 2. Candidate composition dimensions

- Technical: engineering, software/AI, electronics, robotics, communications, cybersecurity, instrumentation, fabrication, systems integration.
- Scientific: physics, biology, chemistry, geology, astronomy, environmental/planetary science, statistics, experimental design.
- Medical/human performance: medicine, emergency care, psychology, neuropsychology, neuroscience, physiology, sleep/circadian science, behavioral health, human factors, team performance.
- Hands-on/trades: mechanical repair, electrical work, construction, machining, fabrication, environmental control/HVAC, agriculture, maintenance, field improvisation.
- Ecological/nature-oriented: fieldcraft, navigation, plants, ecosystems, weather, water/food systems, environmental observation, low-technology resourcefulness.
- Operational: mission planning, logistics, aviation, military operations, emergency response, incident command, safety, remote operations, resource management.
- Creative: art, design, music, visualization, spatial reasoning, divergent thinking, prototyping, unconventional problem-solving.
- Social/relational: mediation, teaching, caregiving, negotiation, leadership, followership, conflict recovery, morale, cross-disciplinary communication.
- Linguistic/cultural: multilingual communication, translation, technical-language transfer, cross-cultural competence, multinational teamwork.
- Analytical: mathematics, statistics, formal reasoning, modeling, systems analysis, causal reasoning, uncertainty estimation, decision science.
- Information/epistemic: research, source verification, provenance assessment, contradiction detection, calibrated confidence, evidence/inference separation.
- Physical: endurance, strength, dexterity, coordination, fine-motor capability, mobility, spatial operation, recovery, and task-specific physical capacity. Requirements should be mission/task based and compatible with accessibility obligations rather than based on one ideal body type.
- Technology orientation: advanced technical users plus people capable of effective operations when technology is degraded or unavailable.
- Experience: specialists, generalists, high-learning-potential novices, technicians, researchers, operators, and cross-domain practitioners.

## 3. Cognitive and behavioral diversity

Avoid simplistic personality labels as qualification criteria. Prefer measurable behaviors and task-relevant tendencies:

- tolerance for ambiguity;
- openness to correction;
- conscientiousness;
- emotional regulation;
- communication under stress;
- conflict recovery;
- willingness to dissent;
- ability to update beliefs;
- curiosity and persistence;
- calibrated confidence;
- ability to lead and follow;
- divergent and convergent problem solving;
- deliberative versus rapid decision tendencies;
- specialist versus generalist reasoning.

Treat these as contextual variables, not permanent identities.

## 4. Crew-level properties

### Capability coverage
Does the team collectively possess required mission capabilities?

### Critical redundancy
Are mission-critical capabilities represented by more than one person, or can another member reach minimum operational competency if the primary specialist becomes unavailable?

### Complementarity
Do strengths compensate for limitations without creating fragile single points of failure?

### Diversity of reasoning
Does the team preserve multiple plausible approaches to unfamiliar problems?

### Communication compatibility
Can different disciplines, languages, cultures, and cognitive approaches exchange actionable information?

### Adaptive leadership
Can task leadership move toward relevant expertise while formal operational authority remains explicit?

### Failure tolerance
Can the crew continue after loss, impairment, isolation, or overload of a member or capability?

### Constructive dissent
Can a minority observer preserve counterevidence when the majority favors another interpretation, without automatic suppression or automatic acceptance?

## 5. Expertise-sensitive dynamic leadership

Candidate role transitions:

- medical emergency → medical expertise takes task lead;
- system failure → engineering/technical expertise;
- environmental/terrain problem → field/geology/environment expertise;
- interpersonal breakdown → behavioral/team expertise;
- cyber or anomalous information condition → security/data/evidence expertise;
- logistics/resource failure → operations/logistics expertise.

Expertise does not automatically equal authorization. Preserve distinctions among subject-matter leadership, recommendation, command authority, and execution authority.

## 6. Language and cultural transfer

Treat multilingual and cross-cultural capability as an operational resource.

Candidate challenge:

language ambiguity → translation → technical terminology → cultural interpretation → reconciliation → operational action.

Measure information loss, translation error, uncertainty preservation, correction latency, and survival of critical meaning.

## 7. Physical capability and crew design

Do not optimize toward one generalized physical ideal. Determine actual physical requirements and ask which capabilities must exist:

1. in every individual;
2. in multiple members for redundancy; or
3. somewhere within the crew collectively.

Distinguish legitimate individual safety requirements from capabilities distributable across the team.

## 8. Low-tech/high-tech resilience

Test whether advanced technical capability is complemented by manual, ecological, mechanical, and low-technology resourcefulness.

Candidate scenario:

normal digital operations → degraded automation → partial sensor loss → communications loss → manual/local reconstruction → restored system → audit.

## 9. Three levels of adaptability

### Individual adaptability
Can an individual learn, recover, transfer knowledge, revise beliefs, and operate safely under uncertainty?

### Crew adaptability
Can a combination of people redistribute roles, preserve critical capabilities, reconcile disagreement, and reorganize when the problem changes?

### Institutional adaptability
Can individuals and teams move among scientific, engineering, civil-space, defense, commercial, emergency-response, educational, and international environments without losing evidence discipline, communication effectiveness, or clarity of authority?

## 10. Institutional and organizational transfer

Potential stakeholder classes:

- civil space;
- defense and military;
- national-security partners where mission requirements and authorities warrant;
- science agencies and national laboratories;
- universities/research institutions;
- community colleges, technical schools, and skilled trades;
- emergency management/infrastructure organizations;
- regulatory, standards, and safety organizations;
- commercial space/technology organizations;
- international partners where authorized.

No organization listed here is implied to participate unless formally designated or partnered.

Different organizations have different languages, incentives, authorities, evidentiary thresholds, decision speeds, information access, risk tolerances, command structures, and failure consequences. These differences can themselves become transfer variables.

## 11. Federated Faculty & Practitioner Corps

The Academy should consider permanent faculty alongside active practitioners, rotating fellows/instructors, technical/trades instructors, operational evaluators, and independent researchers.

Candidate appointment categories:

- Resident Faculty;
- Practitioner Fellow;
- Senior Practitioner;
- Distinguished Fellow;
- Visiting Mission Specialist;
- Technical/Trades Instructor;
- Human Performance/Behavioral Health Specialist;
- Independent Evaluator.

Evaluate candidates on relevant combinations of scientific expertise, operational expertise, engineering/technical expertise, field experience, teaching ability, research quality, innovation, cross-domain capability, mentoring effectiveness, and demonstrated knowledge transfer.

These are conceptual appointment categories, not federal pay grades. Compensation and appointment authority would require separate analysis of applicable personnel authorities and institutional mechanisms.

## 12. Human Performance & Behavioral Health Directorate

Psychology, neuroscience, human factors, behavioral health, and related disciplines should be core mission-support and research capabilities.

Candidate expertise includes operational psychology, clinical psychology where appropriate, neuropsychology, cognitive neuroscience, human factors, industrial/organizational psychology, psychometrics, sleep/circadian science, team dynamics, physiology, aerospace/space medicine, behavioral health, and small-sample/repeated-measures methodology.

Potential functions:

- support participant wellbeing;
- characterize workload, cognition, sleep, stress, team dynamics, and recovery using validated measures;
- help design psychologically safe challenge progression;
- identify human-system interface problems;
- support crew composition and team-development research;
- evaluate transfer and adaptation without reducing participants to permanent psychological labels.

### Required firewall

Care != research != qualification.

Confidential clinical/support information should not casually become qualification data. Optional research participation should not determine advancement. Operational fitness assessments require defined purpose, authority, standards, and safeguards.

## 13. Observer Intelligence role

OI can preserve distinctions among:

observer → observation → interpretation → hypothesis → evidence → contradiction → reconciliation → authority → action → audit.

At the crew level, OI can preserve minority evidence and expertise-sensitive recommendations while maintaining explicit authority boundaries.

At the institutional level, OI can preserve provenance across organizations with different information access and decision authorities.

OI's value must be evaluated comparatively against frozen non-strawman baselines rather than assumed.

## 14. Candidate falsifiable hypothesis

**H5 — Complementary Crew Composition**

Teams selected for measured capability coverage, critical redundancy, cognitive/operational complementarity, and adaptive role allocation will demonstrate greater transfer performance and failure resilience than appropriately matched teams selected primarily by aggregate individual performance.

Candidate endpoints:

- novel-task transfer;
- recovery after capability loss;
- time to role redistribution;
- critical capability coverage;
- information loss across disciplines/languages;
- error detection;
- minority-counterevidence retention;
- workload distribution;
- team coordination;
- decision latency;
- unauthorized action;
- mission/task completion;
- post-challenge recovery.

This hypothesis requires preregistered definitions, appropriate comparators, statistical power analysis, and controls for selection effects.

## 15. Strategic boundary for federal-facing use

This document supports the defensible core: capability qualification, transfer, complementary crew research, human performance, delayed-support/autonomous operations, multidisciplinary prediction, provenance/reconciliation, successive-cohort learning, and integration with existing infrastructure.

Frontier research topics are intentionally maintained in `frontier-research-annex.md` and should not be included in the initial Commission/NASA package or used as evidence supporting this framework.
