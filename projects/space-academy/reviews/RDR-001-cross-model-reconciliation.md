## RDR-001 decisions

### ACCEPT — Federal Core
1. Capability-based operational qualification.
2. Transfer testing into genuinely novel tasks/environments.
3. Delayed-support and autonomous operations.
4. Locked independent multidisciplinary predictions before consensus.
5. Provenance-preserving evidence, contradiction, reconciliation, authorization, and audit.
6. Successive-cohort protocol improvement with preserved null results.
7. Standardized validated human-performance, workload, cognition, sleep, and team measures where applicable.
8. Mapping to NASA-STD-3001 and existing NASA HRP/human-factors infrastructure.
9. Existing or lightly modified analog facilities before capital construction.

### MODIFY BEFORE EXTERNAL USE
1. “Adaptability” must be decomposed into measurable constructs and not treated as a single intuitive score.
2. Adaptive Mentor & Trajectory System must use locked rubrics, multiple evaluators, participant self-assessment, inter-rater reliability checks, and non-punitive revision.
3. Statistical design must account for person × environment × team × training × time × evaluator effects.
4. Data governance must define collection purpose, access, retention, deletion, re-identification risk, and prohibited downstream uses.
5. Research participation must be separated from academic/operational qualification unless participation is independently justified under applicable law and ethics review.

### DEFER / ANNEX
1. Genetic and multi-omics personalization.
2. Family/twin/multigenerational cohort studies.
3. Psychedelic/neuropsychopharmacology research.
4. Large distributed analog-node network.
5. Cleveland vertical-node capital project as a federal pilot requirement.

### FRONTIER REGISTRY — NOT INITIAL FEDERAL PACKAGE
1. Anomalous or nonlocal information-transfer hypotheses.
2. Antipodal/resonance hypotheses.
3. Unconventional consciousness mechanisms.
4. Other low-evidence mechanisms lacking an established aerospace basis.

Retention rule: these hypotheses may remain internally documented as testable hypotheses, but they cannot be used as evidence supporting the Academy architecture. Advancement requires prospective preregistration, ordinary-mechanism controls, leakage audits, adequate statistical design, independent replication, and preservation of null results.

## Pilot sequence

### Pilot 0 — Feasibility
Goal: determine whether the protocol is safe, executable, instrumentable, and auditable.

Required components:
- existing or lightly modified analog/controlled environment;
- standardized baseline measures;
- delayed-support task environment;
- independent expert forecasts locked before outcomes;
- documented immediate-consensus comparator;
- OI provenance/reconciliation layer;
- transfer challenge;
- adverse-event and stopping rules;
- protocol adherence, missing-data, latency, and reconstruction metrics.

Pilot 0 does not establish population-level superiority.

### Pilot 1 — Comparative validation
Goal: test preregistered primary hypotheses with a statistically justified sample.

Sample size is set by power analysis, not model suggestion.

Candidate primary hypotheses:
- H1: preserved independent predictions + provenance-aware reconciliation improve forecast calibration versus immediate consensus.
- H2: transfer-focused qualification predicts novel-task performance better than coursework completion alone.
- H3: OI reduces unsupported or unauthorized execution under adversarial/asymmetric evidence without unacceptable latency.
- H4: successive-cohort protocol updates improve predefined outcomes beyond selection/practice effects.

## Federal narrative decision

The initial federal concept should not argue that the Space Academy needs a new stand-alone analog research ecosystem. It should argue that the Academy can become a national testbed for demonstrable operational capability by integrating existing analog infrastructure with a new qualification/evaluation architecture.

Recommended core statement:

> The United States Space Academy should evaluate not only what participants know, but whether they can maintain sound judgment, team performance, evidence discipline, and operational effectiveness when information is incomplete, communications are delayed, systems are stressed, and the environment is unfamiliar. The proposed architecture adds prospective prediction, transfer testing, provenance-preserving reconciliation, and successive-cohort learning to existing technical and analog training capabilities.
