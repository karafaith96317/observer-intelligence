## Emergency stop and human authority

**What the operator can invoke in the patch.** `SafetyInterlock.stop()` commits a stop flag to the current run database. Reservations sharing that database check the flag inside a write transaction. The relevant boundary is the successful commit. An exception or an unacknowledged stop leaves the outcome unknown. Budget exhaustion and the run deadline also prevent new reservations.

**Proposed shutdown procedure.** An operator may stop immediately on unexpected activity, lost oversight or a policy mismatch. Record the stop request and commit acknowledgement separately. A host supervisor outside the model’s permissions should pause dispatch, cancel queued work, isolate the worker from external tools and verify its actual state. If the latch fails to acknowledge, invoke host isolation directly. Reconcile outstanding permits against observed effects and preserve timestamps and logs. An effect already completed needs incident handling.

**Restart decision.** Keep the stopped run closed. A named operator and a second reviewer should account for outstanding work, record the cause and corrective action, and approve a fresh run with its own target, budget and deadline. Test the host account boundary explicitly. File creation permissions and an operator-only docstring do not establish that a worker cannot replace the policy database.

**Who has authority.** The AI may assemble evidence, flag contradictions and recommend a hold. An accountable human sets the allowed action and can stop it without waiting for a quorum. Independent observers can corroborate a claim using separately collected, time-bound observations. Count common sources once and record dissent. Perceived empathy, a familiar voice, or a biometric score supplies no execution authority. If studying altruistic behavior, use blinded ratings of decisions on consent and harm as a separate research outcome.

## Prioritized validation roadmap

All thresholds below are proposed acceptance criteria. Run tests against temporary records, disposable workers and observable simulated effects. Keep the commands, revision, patch digest, dependency versions, raw output and failures for each run.

| Priority | Test and accountable role | Passing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P0 · Establish the baseline | A test maintainer fixes the tuple fixture so the verifier receives the altered scopes. Pin dependencies and run main, the isolated Tier 0 bundle, and the patched candidate as separate jobs. | All 36 historical main cases reach their intended assertions and pass on the corrected revision. Record both proposal simulations and every collected patch test separately, with exit codes and JUnit results. |
| P0 · Validate local permits | A runtime reviewer tests duplicate requests across threads, processes and restart, wrong target and scope, expired deadline, empty budget, unreadable or locked storage, and worker access to policy files. Include a valid control. | One successful use of the duplicate nonce in the valid-control run, zero repeat grants, zero invalid grants and total grants at or below the budget. Rejections leave the budget intact. A restarted worker cannot clear a stop or restore spent permits. |
| P0 · Drill emergency shutdown | An independent operator triggers stop before reservation, after reservation, at dispatch and during a running job. Repeat with a busy database, unavailable network and supervisor restart. Observe the mock destination independently of OI. | Zero grants after stop commit and zero new effects after confirmed isolation. Account for every pending and in-flight action. Record request-to-commit and request-to-isolation times, worst case and failures. Agree on a maximum safe delay before the drill. A missed bound or unobserved worker fails the drill. |
| P1 · Validate identity and promotion | A hardware reviewer uses enrolled device keys, fresh session challenges, signed PCR selections and digests, and verifier-owned quorum policy. Test key revocation, replay after restart, invalid numeric inputs, wrong architecture and stale or missing evidence. | One independently observed valid device session completes. Every invalid fixture is denied without promotion. Preserve the signed transcript and decision log. Keep hardware identity, location and network activation as separate claims. |
| P1 · Verify history | An evidence reviewer exercises a temporary ledger with valid history, corrupt history, conflicting indexes and interrupted writes. Reconcile Block 42 dates and predecessor provenance. | Existing history stays byte-identical after every rejected write. An accepted test append has a verified predecessor and checksum. Only an independently verified timestamp receipt supports an anchoring claim. |
| P2 · Evaluate ERA and 6G | In simulation, compare local stop alone with local stop plus an authenticated secondary alert channel. Test delay, duplication, loss and a disconnected receiver. Use synthetic telemetry. | Report delivery rate, latency distribution, maximum delay and local-stop success in each condition. Loss of the secondary channel never prevents local isolation or grants permission. Claims about physical location or radio performance need separate measured evidence. |

**What blocks stronger claims today.** The Tier 0 reviewer found no trusted per-node attestation-key binding, incomplete verification of signed PCR data, caller-supplied gate results and quorum, and memory-only replay state. The local patch addresses nonce use for its own simulation path. It supplies no integration evidence for Tier 0. The patch also uses wall-clock time for expiry. Include clock rollback and stale database recovery in testing before relying on deadlines across restarts.

## Independent review before external submission

1. **Freeze the candidate and the claims.** Identify the exact commit, patch digest and intended audience. For each proposed sentence about safety, name the supporting test, execution environment, limitations and reviewer. Label future work explicitly. A research proposal may describe an unresolved question while the corresponding capability stays unverified.

2. **Obtain two independent reviews.** A technical reviewer who did not write the candidate reproduces the applicable tests from a clean environment and retains contradictory results. A separate safety or governance reviewer witnesses the shutdown drill, checks who can stop and restart work, and reviews residual risks. Record names, independence or conflicts, dates, scope and signed decisions. Any disagreement remains visible.

3. **Agree on collaboration and disclosure.** Before sending material through a prospective DARPA collaborator, confirm the specific opportunity or contract route, eligibility, authorized submitter and permitted recipients. Record each contributor’s work, attribution, permission to disclose and approved attachments. Kara approves the exact outgoing version. Contract access, partnership and agency endorsement remain unverified.

4. **Make a claim-specific release decision.** Hold any operational safety claim with an unresolved P0 failure. Require the relevant P1 or P2 evidence for hardware, anchoring or 6G claims. For a proposal limited to research aims, reviewers may approve a narrower package that identifies those gaps, owners and planned tests. Record exactly which version and claims were cleared. External submission is a separate human decision.
