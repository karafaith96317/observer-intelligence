## 1. Neuroimmunology: bone marrow dysfunction in Alzheimer’s disease

### External finding
Abellanas et al. published a *Nature Neuroscience* article on 8 September 2026 reporting impaired bone-marrow myelopoiesis in Alzheimer’s disease, linked in their study to maladaptive type-I interferon signaling. The work included mouse-model experiments and observations in patients.

### Established evidence
- In the 5xFAD mouse model, monocyte differentiation was disrupted.
- Blocking type-I interferon signaling or using IFN-I receptor-deficient bone marrow restored myelopoiesis, normalized monocyte phenotypes, increased monocyte-derived macrophage homing to the brain, and ameliorated disease pathology.
- The authors report a similar monocyte phenotype in patients with Alzheimer’s disease.

### Evidence boundary
The therapeutic intervention was demonstrated in a mouse model. This does not establish clinical efficacy in humans, nor does it demonstrate that peripheral immune mechanisms alone explain Alzheimer’s disease.

### OI / book relevance
This supports a systems-level treatment of human observer state: neural function can be materially affected by peripheral biological processes. It strengthens the case for preserving physiological context as a distinct evidence layer when studying human perception, cognition, or decision-making.

**Do not infer:** that body-state effects establish nonlocal perception, hidden information channels, or observer effects on external physical reality.

### Source
- Abellanas, M. A. et al. “Bone marrow myelopoiesis dysfunction in Alzheimer’s disease limits monocyte homing to the brain and drives disease progression.” *Nature Neuroscience* (2026). DOI: 10.1038/s41593-026-02417-7. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02417-7

## 2. Agentic AI / 6G: intelligent networks moving toward closed-loop autonomy

### External finding
The IEEE Communications Society special issue “Agentic AI for Intelligent Networks” lists a manuscript deadline of 15 September 2026 and describes future wireless systems in which intelligent agents perceive network conditions, make decisions, and coordinate actions with minimal human intervention.

### Established evidence
The call explicitly frames 6G-era networking as increasingly requiring closed-loop, long-horizon, distributed autonomy rather than isolated prediction or optimization.

### Evidence boundary
A call for papers demonstrates an active research direction; it does not establish that any specific agentic architecture is mature, safe, or superior.

### OI relevance
This is a direct external fit for OI’s separation of runtime stages:

```text
Observation -> Inference -> Reconciliation -> Authorization -> Action
```

The strongest falsifiable OI question remains whether provenance-preserving reconciliation plus narrow action authority reduces unsafe or erroneous actions when telemetry is stale, correlated, contradictory, or adversarial.

### Candidate benchmark
Compare a conventional agent loop against an OI-style loop under:
- shared corrupted telemetry,
- stale but resolvable evidence,
- correlated agents masquerading as independent corroboration,
- replayed or expired authority,
- contradictory minority evidence,
- rapid world-state changes.

Measure false authorization, correct escalation, contradiction preservation, provenance completeness, latency, and compute cost.

### Source
- IEEE Communications Society, “Agentic AI for Intelligent Networks,” special issue call for papers. https://www.comsoc.org/publications/journals/ieee-jsac/cfp/agentic-ai-intelligent-networks

## 3. NASA Hubble + Webb: complementary observation of trans-Neptunian objects

### External finding
NASA reported on 8 September 2026 that scientists used the combined capabilities of the Hubble and James Webb Space Telescopes to study trans-Neptunian objects (TNOs), small icy bodies beyond Neptune.

### Established evidence
The joint observations provide compositional and physical information about TNOs and help constrain their formation histories and the conditions of the early Solar System.

### Evidence boundary
Describing these objects as “remembering” the past is metaphorical. The data support historical inference from preserved physical state, not biological or conscious memory.

### OI / book relevance
This is a clean example of complementary observers contributing different evidence channels to one reconstruction problem. It supports the principle that multi-observer systems should preserve instrument/source provenance and uncertainty rather than reduce corroboration to observer count.

### Source
- NASA Science, “NASA’s Hubble, Webb Find Far-out Solar System Objects ‘Remember’ Past,” 8 September 2026. https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-webb-find-far-out-solar-system-objects-remember-past/

## 6. Geopolitics / autonomy: Strait of Hormuz escalation and autonomous underwater systems

### External findings
Reuters reported on 9 September 2026 that Iran and the United States carried out a major series of retaliatory maritime attacks, disrupting shipping near the Strait of Hormuz and pushing energy prices higher. Reuters also reported that Iran said it captured a U.S. Dive-LD autonomous underwater vehicle near the Strait; the Pentagon acknowledged loss of the vehicle and attributed it to a malfunction.

### Established evidence
- Significant reciprocal attacks have occurred around the Strait of Hormuz.
- Shipping traffic has been reduced relative to recent averages.
- The loss/capture of an autonomous underwater vehicle has been acknowledged by the U.S. side, while the two sides differ in framing the event.

### Evidence boundary
Combatant claims about intent, attribution, or operational success should not be treated as established merely because they are reported. Distinguish observed event, source claim, independent corroboration, and later revision.

### OI relevance
This is a strong real-world illustration of state-transition provenance and evidence-validity horizons. A decision justified at time T1 may become invalid at T2 after strikes, losses, route closures, new intelligence, or changed command authority.
