# Observer Intelligence — Collaboration Contribution Ledger

**Status:** Working provenance record — August 2026

## Purpose

This ledger is intended to preserve contribution provenance when Observer Intelligence (OI) is discussed, tested, implemented, or extended with collaborators.

It is not a legal ownership agreement and does not determine patent inventorship, copyright ownership, authorship, or contractual rights. Those questions may require a separate written agreement and, where appropriate, qualified legal advice.

Its research purpose is simpler: record what existed before collaboration, what each participant independently contributed, and what was created jointly so later attribution does not depend on memory alone.

## Contribution classes

### Background contribution
A concept, implementation, document, dataset, benchmark, method, diagram, or claim demonstrably developed by a participant before the collaboration session or project.

### Independent contribution
A new proposal generated by one participant during the collaboration without being derived from another participant's disclosed proposal.

### Joint refinement
A mechanism materially shaped through back-and-forth development by two or more contributors.

### External prior art
A mechanism found in existing literature, software, standards, patents, public systems, or other external work. It should not be attributed to an OI collaborator as original merely because that person introduced the reference to the project.

### Implementation contribution
Code, tests, schemas, benchmarks, infrastructure, documentation, or engineering work that operationalizes a concept without necessarily creating the underlying concept.

## AI-assisted contribution provenance

AI systems should be treated as research tools/reviewers, not reliable arbiters of authorship or legal inventorship.

When an AI output materially changes OI, preserve at least:

- model/system used;
- date;
- prompt or task;
- proposed mechanism;
- whether the proposal was already present in OI;
- whether it was independently reproduced by another reviewer;
- whether external prior art was later found;
- human decision to accept, reject, or modify it.

## Collaboration hygiene

Before major joint implementation or external submission, collaborators should separately resolve in writing, as applicable:

- what each party is bringing into the project;
- treatment of confidential/background material;
- ownership or licensing expectations for jointly created material;
- publication and public-disclosure decisions;
- authorship/credit conventions;
- repository access and commit practices;
- who may submit work externally and under what names;
- what happens if collaboration stops;
- how attribution disputes are handled.

This ledger does not substitute for that agreement.

## Repository practice

Where practical, consequential changes should be made through commits with descriptive messages rather than overwriting history without explanation.

For collaborative research branches, useful commit prefixes include:

```text
KARA:
NICK:
JOINT:
AUDIT:
PRIOR-ART:
BENCHMARK:
DOCS:
```

The prefix is descriptive metadata, not a legal determination of ownership.

## Principle

> Preserve contribution lineage before reconciliation, just as OI preserves evidence lineage before authority.

The goal is fair attribution, reproducibility, and a development history that outside collaborators can inspect.
